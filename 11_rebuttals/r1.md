### **[Q1] The framework may incur false negatives: if the LLM finds a correct repair but fails to produce a valid structured rationale, the repair is discarded. This false negative rate is not quantified.**

### **[A1] Quantifying rejection breakdown and false negative trade-off**

This is constructive, and our framework treats *faithfulness* as a hard constraint: a candidate repair is accepted **only if** its structured rationale compiles into a valid proof trace and all verification backends approve every step. Candidates that are correct but lack a verifiable rationale are rejected by design.

To clarify this trade-off, we decomposed candidate outcomes into four categories across the full evaluation set:

| Outcome Category | Percentage of All Generated Candidates (%) |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Invalid Schema / Parsing Failure | 11.8 |
| Compilation Failure (Proof Trace) | 7.6 |
| Verification Failure (Step-wise) | 23.5 |
| Fully Verified (Accepted) | 57.1 |

To further quantify potential false negatives, we analyzed candidates that produced a semantically correct repaired circuit (as judged by final equivalence checking), but were rejected due to rationale compilation or step-level verification failure.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Repair-Correct but Rationale-Invalid (%) | 4.3 |
| Repair-Correct but Step Verification Failed (%) | 3.7 |
| Total Potential False Negative Rate (%) | 8.0 |

This demonstrates that while some correct repairs are discarded, the magnitude is modest relative to the large gain in semantic precision (96.1%) and faithfulness (94.8%). We will add this breakdown to the camera-ready version to make this trade-off explicit.

Importantly, this design choice is intentional: we prioritize *semantic faithfulness guarantees* over marginal improvements in raw success rate, as the primary reliability risk in quantum APR is accepting spurious but plausible repairs.

We agree that mutation-based bugs do not exhaustively represent all human-authored quantum programming errors.

(ii) Structural mutations such as gate reordering and deletion achieve both high precision and high faithfulness, along with shorter proof traces. (iii) Mutations that induce deeper semantic shifts (e.g., parameter perturbation and qubit index mutation) require longer proof traces and exhibit slightly lower, but still strong, faithfulness. These results indicate that explanation reliability is stable across diverse mutation types and does not collapse under structurally different error patterns.


We appreciate the suggestions regarding the representativeness of mutation-based bugs. While mutation-based evaluation provides controlled semantic ground truth and enables precise measurement of repair correctness and explanation faithfulness, our evaluation is not limited to synthetic perturbations. ## Response to Reviewer pMoH (3/4)

### **[Q4] Dependence on LLM rationale quality may limit robustness.**

### **[A4] Decoupling generation from correctness guarantees**

We appreciate this important concern. Indeed, our framework depends on the LLM to *propose* candidate repairs and structured rationales. However, it does **not** rely on the LLM to guarantee semantic correctness.

Our architecture explicitly separates generation from validation:

| Component | Responsibility |
|------------|----------------|
| LLM | Propose candidate repair + structured transformation rationale |
| Compiler | Convert rationale into executable proof trace |
| Verifier Suite | Validate each transformation step using formal backends |

Semantic correctness is enforced *after* generation by independent verification backends (ZX-calculus, stabilizer reasoning, and quantum simulation). Therefore, accepted repairs must satisfy formal constraints regardless of LLM internal reasoning quality.

### Backbone Sensitivity Analysis

To empirically assess robustness under varying LLM quality, we evaluated our framework across multiple backbone models with substantially different capabilities:

| Backbone | Success Rate (%) | Precision (%) | Faithfulness (%) |
|----------|------------------|---------------|------------------|
| GPT-4o-mini | 76.1 | 96.1 | 94.8 |
| Claude-3.5-Sonnet | 78.9 | 97.4 | 95.9 |
| DeepSeek-R1 | 74.2 | 94.8 | 93.2 |
| Gemini-2.5-Pro | 75.6 | 95.3 | 94.1 |

On the one hand, **success rate varies moderately across models**, reflecting differences in candidate generation quality. On the other hand, **precision and faithfulness remain consistently high (>93%) across all backbones**, including open-weight models. This demonstrates that model capacity primarily affects the *probability of generating a verifiable repair*, rather than the correctness of accepted outputs.

### Controlled Ablation: Disabling Step-wise Verification

To further isolate the role of verification, we conducted a controlled ablation in which the LLM generates structured rationales as usual, final circuit equivalence is checked, **but step-wise proof-trace validation is disabled.** The results based on GPT-4o-mini backbone as bellow:

| Setting | Success Rate (%) | Precision (%) | Faithfulness (%) |
|----------|------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Ours (Full Verification) | 76.1 | 96.1 | 94.8 |
| No Step-wise Verification | 65.4 | 81.7 | 52.6 |

We observe that the success rate decreases, and **precision drops by ~14 points**, and explanation faithfulness collapses. Many accepted repairs are semantically inconsistent at intermediate transformation steps. This confirms that correctness guarantees stem from executable proof-trace verification, not backbone strength.

### Failure Decomposition Under Smaller Models

To understand robustness under weaker generation, we analyze rejection causes with the DeepSeek-R1 backbone:

| Rejection Reason | Percentage of Candidates (%) |
|------------------|------------------------------|
| Invalid structured schema | 12.9 |
| Proof trace compilation failure | 8.7 |
| Step-wise verification failure | 23.4 |

These invalid candidates are filtered before acceptance, preventing hallucinated or partially correct repairs from being counted as successful. Across diverse backbone models, lower-capacity models reduce coverage (success rate), but **accepted repairs remain semantically correct** due to verification, disabling step-wise validation substantially degrades precision and faithfulness. Therefore, LLM rationale quality affects generation efficiency, but semantic robustness is enforced by the independent verification layer. We will clarify this robustness and capacity trade-off and include the controlled ablation results in the manuscript.
